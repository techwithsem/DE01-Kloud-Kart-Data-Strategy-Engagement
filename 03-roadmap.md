# Kloud Kart: 12-month roadmap, From December 2026 to November 2027

The roadmap is designed so that every line names who is better off, what changes for them, and how Nana checks it herself, without asking an engineer for explanations.

NB: Q represents the quarter number. Quarter is every 3 months.

| Quarter (With end goals) | Who, and what changes | How Nana checks it, and by when |
|------------|--------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Q1 · December to February**<br>Stop the losses.<br>Consultant and developer; data engineer joins February | Customers and Nana: Monday reports no longer slow the shop | From Monday 18 January she opens the shop on her phone at 09:00 while her report is prepared. It loads as fast as on a Sunday, and the developer's weekly speed log shows no Monday dip. |
| | Nana and the CEO: the DPC complaint is answered with evidence | By 26 February she signs the reply. Attached is every place the customer's data was held and the date each was cleared, matching the customer data register. |
| | Nana and Finance: one meaning of "revenue" | By 26 February both hold the same signed page: when an order counts, and how refunds, delivery fees and VAT are treated. |
| | The board: Kloud Kart is registered with the DPC | By 31 January she finds Kloud Kart on the DPC's public register with a valid expiry date, and a named manager has started supervisor training. |
| **Q2 · March to May**<br>One set of numbers.<br>Analytics engineer joins April | Nana: no more Monday rebuild | From Monday 3 May her numbers wait in one report she opens at 07:00. By 31 May she has not opened her old spreadsheet for four Mondays. |
| | Nana and Finance: the two revenue figures agree | By 31 May she rings Finance: their April revenue and the report's match to the cedi. |
| | Marketing: every customer list is accounted for | From 1 April every list taken has a line in the register with an owner and a delete-by date. She can say how many copies exist today. |
| **Q3 · June to August**<br>Numbers she can trust | Nana: bad numbers are flagged before she reads them | From 1 July a failed or odd-looking update sends her an email by 07:00. Once a month the engineer breaks a load on purpose and she confirms the email came. |
| | Any customer: deletion is complete and fast | By 31 August a test request is finished everywhere within five working days; the register shows each place and date. |
| | Management: Nigeria visible on its own | By 31 August the report shows Nigeria separately, with under 1 in 100 orders missing a country. |
| **Q4 · September to November**<br>Ready for what comes next | The board: the pack needs no hand work | At the November meeting she can point to the report page behind every figure, and "today's orders" updates at least every 15 minutes. |
| | Customers and Nana: overselling is measured | From 1 September the report shows oversold orders per week since Q1; she sees the line flat or falling. |
| | Nana and the board: a costed next step | By 30 November a two-page paper compares staying on our server with a cloud warehouse, using our own twelve months of figures, and ends in a yes or no. |

**Why this order?** 

That is because Q1 stops loss and legal exposure or problems before anything new is built. The revenue definition comes early because it is cheap and everything after it depends on it. The expensive, hard-to-reverse choice (a cloud warehouse or not) comes last, when there is a year of facts and information to decide it on.
